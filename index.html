Research Background

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Fudan University. During my Ph.D., I focused on Operating Systems and System Architecture. In 2011, I published a paper on SOSP, one of the top conferences in Operating Systems area. It was also the first paper in history since 1967 that is from an Asian university on these top Operating Systems conferences. After graduation, I joined the industry as a system architect. I returned to academia as a researcher with the motivation to push forward the boundary of artificial general intelligence (AGI). With a system research background, I see AI as a "complex system" rather than mathematical functions and algorithms.

Research Interests

My research focuses on artificial general intelligence and embodied robotics driven by an AGI brain. More specifically, I am interested in digging into the root of the learning mechanism in the biological systems and developing models that can live independently in open environments, without pre-programming.

Research Philosophy

My research philosophy is that AGI is not designed, but emerged. Functions can emerge from the basic components of neurons and their connections. High level behaviors and goals can emerge from the dynamics of the system. Our team is following a bottom-up path to build brain models that can give rise to emergent intelligence similar to the human brain.
